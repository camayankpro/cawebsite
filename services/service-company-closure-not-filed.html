COMPANY CLOSURE

Closure of a Company That Has Not Filed Any Forms

Agarwal Mayank & Company — Chartered Accountants Hapur, Uttar Pradesh, India

If your company was incorporated but has not filed its annual returns or financial statements for one or more years, closing it takes more work than a simple strike-off. The ROC requires the company to be brought up to date first. We handle both the catch-up filings and the closure, so you deal with one team.

When This Service Applies

Companies that have not filed their financial statements and annual returns for consecutive years may be removed by the ROC under Section 248 of the Companies Act, 2013. A company can also apply for voluntary strike-off in the same situation. Typical situations include:

  • Annual returns (MGT-7 or MGT-7A) and financial statements (AOC-4 or AOC-4 XBRL) pending for one or more years
  • Company is inactive, with no business and no bank activity
  • Directors have lost track of the company or no longer wish to keep it
  • A notice from the ROC asking for compliance is outstanding

Important: Pending filings attract late fees for each year, and the amount depends on the years missed and the company's capital. We calculate the exact figure during the review, so you know the full cost before you decide.

Our Process, Step by Step

Step 1: Compliance review. We check the company's MCA master data and list every pending filing, year by year.

Step 2: Catch-up filings. We prepare and file the pending annual returns and financial statements, with the late fees payable for each year.

Step 3: Eligibility for strike-off. Once the filings are up to date, we confirm the company has no liabilities and no pending dues.

Step 4: Board and shareholder approvals. We prepare the resolutions and consents needed for the closure application.

Step 5: Application to the ROC. We file the strike-off application through the MCA portal, with the required declarations.

Step 6: ROC notice and objection period. The ROC publishes a notice and allows time for objections. We monitor the portal and respond to ROC queries.

Step 7: Removal from the register. If no valid objection is raised, the company is removed from the register. We confirm the outcome and provide the final documents.

Documents Usually Required

  • Certificate of incorporation, PAN and TAN of the company
  • Digital signature certificates (DSC) of the directors
  • Last filed financial statements, if any, and the company's bank statements for the pending years
  • Director and shareholder details, with identity and address proof
  • Details of any GST registration, income tax filings, or loans and dues that still exist

Timeline

The catch-up filings take a few weeks, depending on how many years are pending and how quickly the documents are provided. After filing, the strike-off process usually takes several months. We give a realistic timeline after the review.

Common Reasons Closures Get Delayed

  • Many years of pending filings, which increase late fees
  • Missing bank statements or accounts for earlier years
  • Directors who are unavailable or cannot provide a valid DSC
  • Unresolved liabilities, such as tax notices or loans still shown in the records
  • GST or income tax registrations still active, which must be closed separately

Frequently Asked Questions

My company hasn't filed for several years. Can I still close it?

Yes, in most cases. The company first needs its pending filings completed, and then it can apply for strike-off. We check the position during the review.

How much are the late fees?

Late fees depend on the number of years pending and the company's share capital. We calculate the exact amount for your company during the compliance review, before any filing is made.

What if the ROC has already sent a notice?

Please share the notice with us. Notices have deadlines, and we respond to them as part of the closure process.

Will the directors face penalties for past non-filing?

Penalties may apply for past non-filing, depending on the years and the company's history. We assess this during the review and explain the position before taking any action.

Is it better to close the company or keep it active?

If the company has no business plans, closure usually stops further compliance cost. If there are plans to restart, keeping it active may be better. We explain both options so you can decide.

Can this be done from anywhere in India?

Yes. The process is handled online through the MCA portal. Clients across India can engage us remotely, and we are based in Hapur, Uttar Pradesh.
