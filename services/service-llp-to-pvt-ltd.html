LLP CONVERSION

Conversion of LLP into Private Limited Company

Agarwal Mayank & Company — Chartered Accountants Hapur, Uttar Pradesh, India

An LLP can be converted into a private limited company when the partners want the structure of a company, such as external investment, share-based funding, or a more formal governance model. The conversion keeps the business running but changes the legal form, so it needs careful planning.

Who Can Convert

Conversion is generally possible where the LLP meets the conditions under the LLP Act, 2008 and the Companies Act, 2013. Typical requirements include:

  • The LLP is in good standing, with all filings up to date
  • There are no pending investigations, prosecutions or proceedings against it
  • The partners agree to the conversion and the required approvals are obtained
  • The company's name, share capital and directors can be set up as required

Important: Conversion affects tax, contracts, bank accounts and employee records. We review these before any filing, so you know what changes and what continues.

Our Process, Step by Step

Step 1: Eligibility review. We check the LLP's filings, liabilities and agreements to confirm it can be converted.

Step 2: Approvals. We prepare the partners' consents and the resolutions needed for the conversion.

Step 3: Name and documents. We prepare the company's name approval and the incorporation documents.

Step 4: Filing with the ROC. We file the conversion application through the MCA portal, with the required forms and declarations.

Step 5: Certificate of incorporation. Once the ROC approves, the company is issued a certificate of incorporation.

Step 6: Transition. We guide you through transferring assets, liabilities, contracts, bank accounts, GST and tax registrations to the company.

Documents Usually Required

  • LLP identification number (LLPIN) and the LLP agreement
  • Partner consents and identity and address proof of each partner
  • Latest filed financial statements and annual returns of the LLP
  • Digital signature certificates of the proposed directors
  • List of assets, liabilities and contracts to transfer

Timeline

The conversion usually takes several weeks to a few months, depending on approvals, the ROC's processing and the transfer of registrations. We give a realistic timeline after the eligibility review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the business continue after conversion?

Yes. The business continues under the company, but the legal form changes. Contracts and registrations are transferred as part of the process.

Do the partners become shareholders?

Partners usually become shareholders in the converted company, in the proportions agreed. We set this out in the conversion plan.

What happens to GST and PAN after conversion?

The company needs its own PAN and GST position. We confirm the changes needed for each registration.

Is there a tax impact on conversion?

Conversion can have tax consequences depending on the structure. We review this before filing and explain the position.
